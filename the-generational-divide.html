The Generational Divide: Are We Asking the Right Questions?

A reflection on examinations, generational assumptions and the need for mutual understanding.

Didula Weerasekara · 28 November 2026 · 2 min read

Why do students seem less enthusiastic about examinations, even when they are just around the corner?

Many teachers attribute this behaviour to the younger generation. Interestingly, I remember hearing much the same argument from teachers when I was at school. The generations have changed, yet the complaint remains remarkably familiar.

Should we not, then, interrogate this assumption and seek to establish whether it is actually true? Otherwise, every new generation risks being unfairly judged by the standards and expectations of the generations that precede it.

Perhaps what we perceive as a lack of enthusiasm is, at least in part, a failure to understand how younger generations think, learn and respond to expectations.

I do not think this is merely about teachers and students. It is about something much broader: the generational divide.

The real question is whether we have done enough to bridge this divide and enable different generations to collaborate towards a common goal.

The answer, I suppose, is no. Had we made a conscious effort to understand one another, perhaps we would not still be having the same conversation decades later.

Rather than expecting younger generations to conform to our expectations, should we not also reconsider how we communicate those expectations?

So, dear fellows, let us bridge the gap. I know that we cannot resolve this issue alone, yet there is something each of us can do.

After all, something is always better than nothing.
